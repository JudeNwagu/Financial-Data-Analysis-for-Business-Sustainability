# Financial-Data-Analysis-for-Business-Sustainability
Analyzed revenue, profitability, liquidity, and efficiency across regions to evaluate financial performance and provide insights for business sustainability

## Introduction
Raw sales and profit data often hides the real story behind business performance. In this project, I uncovered those stories by analyzing company data segmented across markets and regions. Using Excel Power Query, I cleaned, transformed, and prepared raw CSV files for exploration, ensuring the dataset was accurate and ready for insights. Guided by the six stages of the data analysis process: Ask, Prepare, Process, Analyze, Share, and Act. I revealed revenue trends, profitability gaps, and efficiency patterns. The outcome highlights actionable strategies that drive growth and support long-term business sustainability

---

 ## Ask

 **Problem Statement**

 Ask Stage: Defining the Business Problem
The first step focused on understanding the business challenge and framing the right questions to guide the analysis. The central objective was:

"How is the company performing financially across different market segments and regions, and where can improvements be made to boost profitability and efficiency?"

From this, I developed key guiding questions:

* Which market segments and regions generate the highest sales and profits?

* Where are underperforming areas that may need attention?

* What trends exist in revenue and profit over time?

* How can these insights support smarter decision-making?

This stage ensured the analysis was aligned with business goals and that the insights delivered would be both relevant and actionable.

---

## Prepare

**Data Collection and Understanding**

In this stage, I gathered and reviewed the dataset to understand its structure, variables, and relevance to the business objectives. The dataset was imported from a CSV file into Power Query in Microsoft Excel, where I carried out initial exploration.

The dataset contains 22 columns and 638 rows, covering sales and profit data across countries, market segments, and time periods.

Key Variables:

Segment – Business or customer category (e.g., Consumer, Corporate, Home Office).

Country – Geographic location of the sale.

Product – Specific product sold.

Discount Band – Category of discount applied (e.g., None, Low, High).

Units Sold – Quantity of product sold.

Manufacturing Price – Production cost per unit.

Sale Price – Selling price per unit before discounts.

Gross Sales – Total revenue before discounts (Sale Price × Units Sold).

Discounts – Value of discounts given.

Sales – Net sales amount after discounts.

COGS (Cost of Goods Sold) – Total cost to produce units sold.

Profit – Net gain from sales (Sales – COGS).

Date / Month / Year – Transaction time details.

Profit Margin – Profit percentage from sales (Profit ÷ Sales).

Average Revenue per Unit – Revenue earned per unit sold.

Discount Rate – Percentage discount applied.

This stage confirmed the dataset’s completeness and relevance, providing a strong foundation for transformation and deeper analysis.

---


## Process

**Data Tranformation**


In this stage, I focused on preparing the dataset to ensure accuracy, consistency, and reliability for analysis. The sales and profit data (imported as CSV files) was cleaned and transformed in Power Query (Excel) to create a structured dataset.

Key transformation steps:

* Standardized column names by correcting errors and inconsistencies at import.

* Removed unwanted characters (e.g., “$”, “–”) to ensure clean values.

* Converted data types from text to integers or decimals for accurate calculations.

* Removed commas from numeric fields so they were properly recognized as numbers.

These steps resulted in a well-structured, analysis-ready dataset that ensured accurate insights during the later stages.

##  Analyze

**Exploring the Data and Generating Insights**

At this stage, I utilized Excel visualizations and dashboards to explore the cleaned dataset, uncovering trends, patterns, and relationships in the company’s financial performance. The analysis focused on the Government segment (2013), with insights derived from key metrics and visual breakdowns.

**📌 Key Performance Insights (2013 – Government Segment):**

* Total Profit: $8.5M (+195% vs. LY)

* Sales Revenue: $39.4M (+201% vs. LY)

* Profit Margin: 29.2% (slightly down –1% vs. LY)

* COGS: $30.9M (+203% vs. LY)

* Units Sold: 347.2K (+181% vs. LY)

* Average Revenue per Unit: $116.3 (–1% vs. LY)

* Total Discounts: $3.0M (+224% vs. LY)


** Trend & Performance Highlights:**

* Seasonality: Sales and profit peaked in October and December, showing seasonal spikes in government purchases.

* Top Markets: Germany led with $5.7M sales and $1.1M profit, followed by the USA, France, Canada, and Mexico.

**Product Analysis:**

* Highest Sales/COGS: Paseo (~$16M COGS) and Amarilla.

* Highest Discounts: Velo ($536.6K) and Paseo ($527.2K).

  **Profit Margins:** 

  *VTT (40.3%), Paseo (37.4%), and Velo (35.2%) performed best, while Amarilla (18.9%) flagged a pricing/cost challenge.

Takeaway: The Government segment showed strong year-over-year growth, but discount-heavy products and margin erosion in Amarilla highlight areas for optimization.
